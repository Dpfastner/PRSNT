# PRSNT — Name Candidates

`prsnt.io` is taken (existing gift-giving site), so the codename "PRSNT" must be replaced before any brand investment.

This doc is a **brainstorming shortlist** — none of these names have been availability-checked yet. The next step is to filter against `.com`, App Store, social handles, and trademark databases (USPTO basic search), then narrow to 3 and test with target users.

## Criteria recap (from `docs/STRATEGY.md` §0)

- Available everywhere: `.com` (or strong alt TLD), iOS App Store, Google Play, Instagram, TikTok, X
- Trademark-clear in launch geography
- Searchable: "<name> gift app" returns *you* on Google
- Pronounceable: real word or near-word
- Carries the irreverent voice (it's the moat)
- **Avoid the gift-pun graveyard:** Giftly, Gifted, Giftster, Wantist, Elfster all already exist. Lean away from "gift-/give-/wish-" prefixes.

## Candidates

### Action / verb (what the app does for you)

| Name | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Snag** | "I snagged the perfect gift." Punchy, casual, voice-fit. | Probably common — verify trademark |
| **Pluck** | Physical, fun, distinctive. Suggests reaching in and finding the right thing. | Less obvious gift connection |
| **Wink** | Playful, conspiratorial, fits the surprise/secret theme. | Probably taken in app space |
| **Tipoff** | You got a tipoff about the perfect gift. Fits Secret Santa connotation. | Sports-betting connotation |
| **Knack** | "She has a knack for gifts." Implies skill, warm. | Less punchy |
| **Score** | "I scored a great gift." Sports/win flavor. | Common, hard to own |
| **Land** | "You landed a great gift." Short, punchy. | Real-estate associations |
| **Aced** | "Aced the gift." | Alphabet/Apple already has Ace branding |

### Surprise / secret-keeper angle

| Name | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Sealed** | Meta-clever (matches the "Sealed" privacy tier in our data model). Envelope/secret implication. | Common dictionary word |
| **Stash** | Your stash of gift ideas. Slightly subversive, voice-fit. | Drug connotations could read wrong |
| **Hush** | Secret-keeper, warm. | Quiet feels passive for an action app |
| **Whisper** | "A whisper told me you'd love this." Strong narrative fit. | Whisper app brand exists (different space) |
| **Cache** | Your gift cache. Tech-savvy double meaning. | Pronunciation ambiguity (cache vs cachet) |

## Top 8 shortlist (my picks before availability check)

Ranked by voice-fit + brandability + gift-relevance:

1. **Snag** — "I snagged it." Active, casual, voice-fit, gift-natural.
2. **Sealed** — Meta-clever (privacy-tier match), envelope/secret narrative, dignified.
3. **Wink** — Playful, conspiratorial, the perfect emotional beat.
4. **Tipoff** — Secret Santa-native, narrative-rich.
5. **Boo** — Form-native, weird-distinctive, pet-name doubles as love-language hook.
6. **Wren** — Warm person-name, "a little birdie" gift trope, brandable.
7. **Stash** — Subversive voice-fit, but flag drug-connotation testing.
8. **Pluck** — Physical, distinctive, fun to say.

## Next steps

1. **Availability sweep** — run all 8 through namechk.com, App Store search, USPTO TESS, Google for direct conflicts.
2. **Drop the dead names** — anything with a `.com` parking page selling for $XX,XXX or an existing app in the same space.
3. **Narrow to 3** — pick the survivors that still carry voice + are clean.
4. **User test** — show 10 people in the target demo (gift-givers, family organizers): "Which would you download? Which sounds like a gift app you'd trust?"
5. **Lock the name** — register `.com`, App Store name, all social handles, file trademark intent-to-use.

Until then, **PRSNT remains the codename** in the repo, code, and internal docs. Do not invest in a logo or marketing site against PRSNT.
